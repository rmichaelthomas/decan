## Design Context

### Users

Decan's public interface is for standards-minded builders, scheduling-system maintainers, agent developers, protocol designers, and technically curious operators who have felt the limits of cron, RRULE, calendar APIs, and workflow triggers. They arrive needing to understand whether Decan is a toy DSL, a runtime package, a proposed standard, or a practical bridge between human temporal intent and machine execution.

### Brand Personality

Audacious, exacting, humane. Decan should feel like a serious primitive with a sharp point of view: calm enough to trust, strange enough to remember, and precise enough that a standards person does not feel marketed at.

### Aesthetic Direction

Editorial-standard rather than SaaS-dashboard. Use light, paper-like surfaces, dense but breathable typography, rule lines, marginalia, and deliberate asymmetry. Avoid neon agent-culture gloss, generic developer-card grids, fake metrics, and empty futurism.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with the primitive: Decan is the layer before calendars, schedulers, and agents act.
2. Show evidence early: every claim should connect to source, canonical form, context, resolution, or replay.
3. Preserve human legibility: examples should read like documents, not config sludge.
4. Preserve agent operability: every human-facing claim should have a CLI/MCP counterpart.
5. Stay audacious without overclaiming: present Proper Time as a standards-shaped proposal and Decan as the reference implementation, while naming non-goals plainly.
